The short version

Views cluster on four things evoila already sells: the VCF 9 upgrade and design path, AI-era security, networking, and private AI. Hyperscaler-hosted VMware (Azure VMware Solution, AWS, Oracle) drew the least interest of any topic.

Volume is low. The typical session has fewer than 50 views after about 12 weeks online, so treat gaps of 10 or 20 views as noise. The gaps worth acting on are the 2x and 3x ones.

What this means for our content

1

Lead with the VCF 9 path. It is the biggest overlap.

Platform design and what's-new sessions averaged 151 views, and upgrade and migration sessions 84. "Design & Architect: Private Cloud Deployment" (289) and "Choosing the Right Path to VCF 9: Upgrade, Converge, Import, or Rebuild" (190) were the top how-to sessions. This is our plan, build and run core.

2

Frame security through AI threats, not product features.

AI-era security sessions averaged 138 views, against 58 for conventional vDefend, hardening and zero-trust sessions. "Operationalizing Cyber Defense for the Agentic AI Era" drew 232, while the step-by-step vDefend tutorial drew 33. Same substance, with AI attackers as the hook.

3

Networking is the sleeper.

Networking sessions averaged 85 views with a median of 73, one of the steadiest topics in the set. "What's New in VCF 9.1 Networking" (153), the VPC tutorial (126) and the Arista fabric deep dive (121) all did well. It matches the "you don't have to redesign your network to move platforms" message.

4

Private AI: the interest is in the big announcements, not yet the how-to.

Private AI had the most sessions and the most total views, but its median session drew 47, the same as a typical session. The total comes from Broadcom's launches: "Introducing VMware AI Factory" (341) and "AI-Ready Private Cloud with Cisco" (282). Content should explain what changes and what it costs, not deep GPU tuning.

5

Hardware cost and memory tiering is an easy win.

Hardware economics sessions averaged 72 views. "Avoiding the RAM-pocalypse" (161) and "Conquer the 2026 Hardware Crisis" (122) beat most product sessions. It sits under the licensing-economics side of the platform practice.

6

Weak spots to weigh before investing content time.

Hyperscaler-hosted VMware was the lowest topic at 28 views per session, and all four Azure VMware Solution sessions drew under 40, so AVS content may need a different hook than Explore offered. Tanzu app platform (31) and AI developer tooling (49) were also low. Kubernetes sat in the middle (61); its best sessions were hands-on, like "Kubernetes Basics to GitOps" (164).

How to pitch, based on the 2026 views

  • Pitch the topics people watched. Platform and what's-new sessions averaged 151 views, AI-era security 138, networking 85, VCF 9 upgrade and migration 84. The typical session drew 47.
  • Put a Broadcom speaker or a named customer on stage. Sessions with only partner speakers had a median of 30 views, against 55 with a Broadcom speaker.
  • Tutorials draw the most per session. Median 81 views, against 33 for quick talks. They are also the most prep.
  • Name the release. Titles with "What's New", "9.1" or "Introducing" had a median of 84, against 45 for all other titles. The titles below use 9.x since the release may move by May.
  • Lead with a use case, not a capability pitch. Vendor-pitch sessions sat near the bottom of the list.

If we submit only two: Tom's AI-era security session and Andrew's upgrade-path session. They sit on the two strongest overlaps between audience interest and what we deliver.

How this was built

View counts come from all 190 active videos in the VMware Explore video library, pulled November 12, 2026. Every video is from Explore Las Vegas 2026, and nearly all were posted August 21, 2026, so each has had about the same time to collect views.

VMware's own tags are too coarse to use (129 of 190 videos are "Cloud Infrastructure"), so each session got one topic by reading its title, product tag and speakers. Some calls could go either way.

evoila fit: High is a core practice (platform, Kubernetes, networking, security, private AI, managed operations). Medium is adjacent work inside those engagements (recovery, storage, automation, hardware and licensing cost, Azure VMware Solution). Low is outside what we sell today. Treat fit as a working assumption the team can adjust.

Limits: these are on-demand plays on vmware.com only, not live attendance, YouTube re-posts or slide downloads. The general session keynote is excluded from topic averages.
